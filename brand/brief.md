# Porchlight: brand brief

Draft 1, 2026-10-23. Research behind it: [`research.md`](research.md). Lines marked **Decide** are open; everything else is a proposal to react to.

## 1. Background

Porchlight is a home page for each member of a family, where every card is written by AI for that person: their day, their calendar, the weather where they live, things happening nearby, articles on what they care about, and any card they describe in a sentence. Parents set it up, invite the family and look after the kids' pages. v1 is the household's own daily tool. v2 turns it into a family AI agent and, later, a product other families pay for.

The look so far grew out of the design interview: "a small room with a light left on, not a dashboard." It uses Gambarino and Switzer, warm off-white surfaces, an indigo UI color and an amber dot as the "light." It has no logo beyond that dot, no dark theme and no written identity.

## 2. The job of this brief

Give Porchlight an identity that:

1. a family member would recognize from across the kitchen, on a phone, a laptop or a wall display;
2. no competitor already has (the category has converged on cream, a soft serif and an orange button);
3. stays warm in a dark theme;
4. can grow from a household tool into a product without a redesign;
5. survives a change of name, if the trademark question below forces one.

## 3. Audience

| Who | What they need from the brand |
| --- | --- |
| **Parents** (primary; they set it up and pay) | Calm and trust. It should feel like it belongs in their home and treats their kids' data carefully. No ads, nothing childish, nothing corporate. |
| **Teens, about 12 to 17** | Their page should feel like theirs, not like a chore app their parents installed. Not babyish. |
| **Younger kids, about 6 to 11** | Big, clear, friendly. Something to tick off and finish. Readable before they're strong readers. |
| **Extended family** (grandparents invited as members) | Legible, unfussy, obvious. |

## 4. The idea

**The light's on.**

A porch light means someone's home and you're expected. Porchlight is the light a family leaves on for each other.

It works through the two moments that frame a family's day:

- **Morning: off the porch.** Everyone gets what they need for the day and heads down the steps. The morning briefing, today's calendar and the routine are the steps.
- **Evening: the light comes on.** Everyone comes home, and the porch is where the day gets talked about. (v2: the family agent sums up everyone's day for the evening.)

**Positioning, draft:** For families who want to start the day ready and end it together, Porchlight is a home page for each person that the family's own AI keeps up to date. Other family organizers manage a schedule. Porchlight greets you at the door.

**Decide:** whether the evening moment is a v2 feature to design for now, or only a brand story for later.

## 5. Personality

| Porchlight is | It isn't |
| --- | --- |
| Warm, like a lamp, not like a sale | Saccharine, cute, emoji-heavy |
| Neighborly: plain words, unhurried | Corporate, clinical, "productivity" |
| Crafted: wood, brass, paint, hand-made | Gadget-glossy, neon, techy gradients |
| Quietly clever: the AI is felt, not shown off | "Powered by AI" sparkles everywhere |
| Steady and trustworthy with kids | Surveillance, alarms, red badges |

## 6. Brand assets to own

The distinctive things that should mean Porchlight wherever they appear. Each is a design system element, not a decoration.

1. **The glow.** Light with real falloff: a warm core fading to nothing. It marks what is alive or new (a card that just arrived, the AI thinking, the briefing). Amber is a light source in this system, not a button fill.
2. **Haint blue.** Southern porches paint their ceilings a pale blue-green, said to keep spirits (and wasps) away. It's the everyday UI color, replacing indigo: calm, specific, never used by a tech brand. It pairs with amber as complements. The tradition comes from the Gullah Geechee people of the Lowcountry; if we tell its story in marketing, credit that origin.
3. **Wood.** Grain as a faint texture on brand surfaces (sign-in, empty states, the sidebar), never behind content. Porch boards as a layout rhythm: horizontal bands with a fine seam between them.
4. **The steps.** Three steps down from the porch: the shape of any morning sequence (a routine, a checklist, onboarding). Finishing the last step is the "off you go" moment.
5. **The day cycle.** The theme follows the family's day. "Daybreak" is the light theme; "Porch light" is the dark theme and the brand's hero state, warm walnut rather than blue-black.

## 7. Visual direction

### Logo

Five concepts are drawn in the branding kit. Requirements for whichever moves forward:

- reads at 16px (favicon) and in one color (embroidery, stamps, a monochrome display);
- works as an app icon on a square and on a circle;
- works on both themes without a second drawing;
- doesn't depend on the word "porchlight" to make sense, so it outlives a rename;
- pairs with a wordmark that can stand alone.

**Decide:** which one or two concepts to develop.

### Typography

| Role | Proposal | Today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display | **Fraunces**, with its "soft" axis turned up: rounded, carved-wood serifs; optical sizes from big headlines down to card titles. | Gambarino (one weight only) |
| Body and UI | **Figtree**: round, open shapes kids can read; tabular figures for times. | Switzer |

Both are free on Google Fonts, so no Fontshare dependency.

**Decide:** move to Fraunces + Figtree, or keep Gambarino + Switzer and spend the distinctiveness on color and the logo. (Fraunces is popular, so the soft axis and how the wordmark is drawn are what keep it ours.)

### Imagery and illustration

- Illustration over stock photos of families: a porch at different hours, simple flat shapes, wood and paint textures, no faces. Every family can see itself in it, and it costs nothing to make more.
- If photos are used later: real evening light, lamps, porches, hands; never staged kitchen smiles.

### Motion

- Light fades up, it doesn't pop. Cards arrive the way a lamp warms, about 500ms, an ease-out.
- The thinking state is a breathing glow, not a spinner.
- Finishing the last step of a routine gets one small celebration, then gets out of the way.
- Everything respects reduced-motion settings.

## 8. Voice

Talk like a good neighbor on the porch: plain, warm, specific, brief.

| Moment | Say | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning greeting | "Morning, Maya. 58° and clearing up, soccer at 4." | "Here's your AI-powered daily digest!" |
| Routine done | "That's everything. Off you go." | "Task complete! +10 points 🎉" |
| Empty page | "Nothing here yet. Tell Porchlight what you'd like to see." | "No widgets configured." |
| Error | "The weather didn't load. Try again in a minute." | "Error 503: upstream timeout." |
| Kid-safe marker, to a parent | "Picked with Jordan's age in mind." | "Content filtered." |

Never: "mental load," "in sync," "supercharge," "seamless," "powered by AI."

## 9. Where it shows up

In order of need:

1. Favicon and app icon
2. Wordmark and in-app header lockup (replacing today's dot + "Porchlight")
3. Design tokens for both themes, as Tailwind `@theme` variables in `frontend/src/styles/index.css`
4. Sign-in, family setup and empty states (the first places the wood and the porch illustration appear)
5. The morning briefing and routine cards (the glow and the steps)
6. Demo video title card and README header
7. Later: marketing site, social, a wall-display mode

## 10. Constraints

- **The name.** A pending PORCHLIGHT trademark application (serial 50045511, filed 2026-08-12) covers AI family-briefing software. See the research. Get a clearance search before spending on name-bound assets such as a domain, a drawn wordmark or printed material.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG AA contrast in both themes. Nothing relies on color alone. Kid-facing text at 16px or larger.
- **Kids.** No dark patterns, no streak guilt, no ads, ever.
- **Fonts.** Free for commercial use and self-hostable.
- **The build.** Everything maps to Tailwind tokens in one file; the dark theme is a set of token overrides, not a second stylesheet.

## 11. How we'll know it works

- Shown the icon alone, someone who has used Porchlight for a week names it.
- Set beside Cozi, Skylight, Hearth and Ohai, it isn't mistaken for any of them.
- The dark theme feels like evening at home, not like a code editor.
- A 9-year-old can find and finish their morning steps without help.
- The identity still works with a different word in the wordmark.

## 12. Open decisions

1. **The name.** Round 1 is in [`naming.md`](naming.md): Kinlight first, with Lamplit and Stoopside as backups. Next is an attorney's clearance search on Kinlight before buying kinlight.com.
2. **Logo concept.** Decided 2026-10-23: the lantern (concept 1), refined in [`logo/`](logo/) with the kinlight wordmark. How it shapes the product is in [`lantern-ux.md`](lantern-ux.md).
3. **Typefaces:** Fraunces + Figtree, or keep Gambarino + Switzer.
4. **Dark theme first?** Make "Porch light" the default in the evening (automatic by time of day), or follow the device setting only.
5. **The evening moment:** design for it now, or keep it as a story for v2.
6. **Illustration:** commission a porch scene, or build it in-house from simple shapes.
