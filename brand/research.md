# Porchlight: competitive research

First pass, 2026-10-23. Covers how the family-organizer category looks and talks today, what families respond to, and where Porchlight can stand apart. It feeds [`brief.md`](brief.md).

Most review sources in this category are affiliate blogs or vendor pages. Where a claim rests on one of those, it says so.

## The name: read this first

**A pending US trademark application for PORCHLIGHT covers software very close to ours.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Mark | PORCHLIGHT (standard characters, so it covers the word in any style) |
| Serial no. | 50045511 |
| Owner | Caraeff Studios LLC, Austin, Texas |
| Filed | 2026-08-12, intent to use (no use claimed yet) |
| Status | Live, awaiting examination (checked on USPTO TSDR, 2026-10-23) |
| Classes | 9 (downloadable software) and 42 (SaaS) |
| Goods | Software giving parents AI-written, plain-language summaries of their children's wellbeing and daily activities, periodic family briefings, child-safety alerts and family account management |

Porchlight's first commit is 2026-10-09, eight weeks after that filing. Their goods describe an AI family briefing, which is what our morning briefing card does. If their application proceeds, it is likely to block a registration for Porchlight in the same classes, and it could make commercial use risky.

The same owner also filed PORCHLIGHT (serial 50046721) in class 45 for child-safety information services. The USPTO lists 29 live marks containing PORCHLIGHT in all, including a registered one in class 9 (Front Porch, Inc., network software) and class 42 software from Porchlight Rental Service. Round 1 of alternatives is in [`naming.md`](naming.md).

Also in the space:

- **Porchlight Connect** (2017, Colorado Springs): a family-connection app that later moved into HIPAA-compliant family and healthcare messaging. No trademark record found.
- **PorchLight Design Studio:** an unrelated iOS graphics app.
- **Porch** (porch.com): home services and insurance. Similar name, different market.

**What this means for the brand work:** this isn't legal advice, and a trademark attorney's clearance search is the real answer. Until then, the brief builds the identity on ideas the name only points at (the light left on, the porch as a threshold, the steps out in the morning) so the visual system survives a rename. See "Open decisions" in the brief.

## What the category looks like right now

These are the defaults. A newcomer that uses them disappears into the shelf.

1. **Daylight only.** Every site is a bright morning kitchen: cream, peach or white grounds. Nobody shows the evening, when families are actually together.
2. **Cream, a soft serif and an orange button.** Skylight, Ohai and (through its photography) Hearth all land here. Our current build (Gambarino on `#fafaf7`, amber accent) sits inside this cluster too.
3. **Teal or navy as the trust color.** Skylight, Hearth and Cozi all lean on a blue-green or blue.
4. **"In sync," "simplified," "mental load."** The copy sells relief from logistics. It's about the parent's burden, rarely about the family's time together.
5. **Photos of real families in kitchens.** Effective, expensive, and interchangeable.
6. **The product is a schedule.** Calendars, lists, chores. The emotional promise is "less chaos."

## What families respond to

- **Kids doing it themselves.** Hearth's most-quoted praise is kids ticking off their own routines. Visual steps, streaks and a sense of finishing.
- **Something everyone sees.** Skylight and Hearth win by living in a shared place (the kitchen wall). Porchlight's per-member pages need a shared moment to compete.
- **Relief without nagging.** Ohai's testimonials center on the parent's head feeling lighter.
- **No ads, nothing tacky.** Cozi's ads are its most common complaint. A family tool earns trust by feeling like the home's, not a billboard.
- **Warmth that looks like the house.** The best-received hardware is the wood and brass frames, things that look like furniture, not gadgets.
- **Easy for everyone.** One older survey found 20% of parents call family tools "too complicated." Ease across ages comes up in every buying guide.

## Where Porchlight can stand apart

| The category does | Porchlight can |
| --- | --- |
| Morning kitchen, daylight only | Own **the evening**: the light coming on, the family home. And own the threshold in the morning: stepping off the porch. |
| Flat cream and orange | Make **light itself** the material: a real glow with falloff, not a flat orange button. Pair it with **haint blue**, the pale blue-green Southern porches paint their ceilings, a detail no tech brand uses. |
| Dark mode as an afterthought | Make the dark theme the **hero** ("porch light on"), warm walnut rather than blue-black. |
| Gadget-clean surfaces | Bring in **wood**: grain as a quiet texture, porch boards as a layout rhythm. The hardware brands proved families want furniture, not tech. |
| "In sync," "mental load" | Talk like a neighbor on the porch: plain, unhurried, a little affectionate. Sell the family's time together, not the parent's to-do list. |
| A schedule with AI bolted on | Every card is alive and written for this person. The morning briefing is a greeting at the door, not a notification. |
